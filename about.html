Why We Exist

Maqam was born from a feeling many young people know but rarely name: the feeling of living between languages. Of thinking in Arabic, dreaming in French, writing in English — and never being told that this is not a confusion, but a gift.


Literature in Arabic, English, and French is too often treated as three separate worlds. But inside the minds of young people across the Arab world and its diaspora, these languages do not compete — they coexist. They fight for the same thought, reach for the same feeling, fall short in the same places.


We started Maqam because we wanted a space where language is not a barrier, but a bridge. Where a poem can exist in three tongues without apology.

Who We Write For

Maqam is for the student who reads Dostoevsky and Al-Mutanabbi in the same week. For the writer who code-switches in WhatsApp messages and calls it natural. For anyone who has ever felt that literature should not be locked inside academic walls, but should breathe in the real world — online, in streets, in thoughts, in conversations.

What We Stand For

One thing, above all: freedom of expression. Not just political freedom — but intellectual and artistic freedom. The right to write what you feel, in the language you feel it in, without fear of being "wrong" or "too much."


Maqam stands for voice. For identity. For the radical idea that literature is a living space — and that all languages, all emotions, all contradictions are welcome here.

Our Three Pillars
I صوت Voice

Every writer has a station — a maqam — from which they speak. We are here to help you find yours, and to make sure it is heard.

II هوية Identity

You are not one language. You are not one culture. You are the sum of everything you have read, spoken, felt, and refused to forget.

III حرية Freedom

Write what you feel, in the language you feel it. Maqam does not grade your expression — it celebrates it.

Your Voice Belongs Here

Maqam is built on the writing of people who have something real to say. We do not ask for credentials or academic titles. We ask for honesty, depth, and the courage to put your thoughts into words.

Write in English, Arabic, French — or all three at once. We will meet you where you are.

◆ What We Publish

Long-form essays, literary criticism, poetry, personal narratives, cultural analysis, and philosophical reflection.

◆ Languages

Arabic, English, and French — or a mix. We celebrate the movement between languages, not just within them.

◆ Who Can Submit

Anyone. Students, independent writers, emerging voices. No academic affiliation required. Only a genuine perspective.

◆ Length

800 to 4,000 words. We value depth over brevity — but we also value every word earning its place.
